## 1. Monthly trends (Analysis 1)

- Total net sales in window: **£114,204.50** (gross £131,780.16 − discounts £6,837.70 − refunds £10,737.96)
- Highest net-sales month: **November 2024, £10,908.65**; lowest: **January 2024, £1,772.98**
- Discounts as % of gross sales over the window: **5.2%**
- Refunds as % of checkout value over the window: **8.6%**
- Share of net sales from returning customers, first 6 months vs last 6 months: **37.4% vs 61.4%**
- Interpretation: Net sales climbed through 2024 as the customer base grew, peaked
  in November–December, then settled around £7,300–£8,600 a month in 2025.
  Returning customers went from about a third of revenue to about three fifths.
  The June 2025 dip (−31.5%) is partly an edge effect: 10 orders from the final
  week are still `pending`, and fewer new customers sign up that month.
- Caveat: growth partly reflects a customer base that starts at zero; the
  Oct–Dec uplift is a generator rule.

## 2. Category and product performance (Analysis 2)

- Top category by net sales: **home_cleaning, 27.8%**
- Category with the largest gap between unit share and revenue share:
  **snacks_beverages** (19.7% of units but 9.3% of net sales). Kitchen shows the
  opposite pattern (9.6% of units, 16.5% of net sales).
- Number of products making up 80% of net sales: **26 of 45**
- Interpretation: Revenue is fairly concentrated at the top. The Cordless
  Handheld Vacuum alone contributes 15.4% of net sales, nearly three times the
  next product (Wireless Earbuds, 5.4%). Low-priced consumables sell in volume
  but add little revenue.
- Caveat: revenue is not profit; `unit_cost` is not used yet.

## 3. Average order value (Analysis 3)

- Overall AOV: **£54.16**; median order value: **£38.98**
- AOV range across months: **£46.13 (Aug 2024) – £58.95 (Jan 2024)**
- Category with the highest average full-basket value: **kitchen, £72.54**
- Interpretation: The mean is well above the median, so a minority of large
  baskets pulls AOV up. AOV is fairly stable month to month; the summer 2024 dip
  is the only clear low point. Orders containing kitchen or home-cleaning items
  have the largest baskets.

## 4. Repeat purchase (Analysis 4)

- Repeat-purchase rate, all purchasing customers: **62.0%** (548 of 884)
- By segment: budget **52.8%**, mainstream **66.1%**, premium **75.2%**
- 90-day repeat rate (eligible customers only): **57.1%** (428 of 750)
- Average days to second order: **44.1**
- Caveat: right-censoring for late joiners. The segment ordering reflects the

## 5. Cohort retention (Analysis 5)

- Typical month-1 retention: **about 25–38%** (full range 20.6–44.4%)
- Typical month-3 retention: **about 13–25%** (full range 8.8–30.2%)
- Cohorts that stand out and why: The **September–November 2024** cohorts have
  the strongest early retention (month 1 at 34.6–44.4%), probably because their
  next months fall in the Q4 peak. The **January 2024** cohort is the weakest
  (20.6% at month 1, 0% at month 12).
- Caveat: late cohorts are small; NULL means "not yet observable".

## 7. Discounts (Analysis 7)

- Avg checkout value, discounted vs full-price orders: **£52.50 vs £54.91**
  (before the discount, discounted baskets are larger: £61.99 gross)
- Does the gap hold within each customer segment (7c)? **No.** Discounted orders
  are smaller for budget (£50.57 vs £56.71) and premium (£52.52 vs £59.35)
  customers but larger for mainstream (£54.40 vs £51.99).
- Monthly correlation, discount share vs net sales: **0.29** (weak positive,
  18 months)
- Statement: this is an association only. Discounts cluster in November and
  among budget customers, and the generator makes promo baskets slightly
  larger by design. An A/B test would be needed to estimate a causal effect.

## 8. Returns (Analysis 8)

- Overall unit return rate: **4.8%** (383 of 7,905 units)
- Products flagged "high sales, high returns": **Wireless Earbuds (32.6%),
  Cordless Handheld Vacuum (21.1%), Smart Plug Twin Pack (16.4%), Pet Bed Medium
  (10.6%), Power Bank 10000mAh, Stainless Saucepan Set, Phone Stand Adjustable,
  Laptop Sleeve 14in**
- Most common return reason in the highest-return category
  (electronics_accessories): **changed_mind (39.1%)**, then not_as_described (30.4%)
- Caveat: Wireless Earbuds, Cordless Handheld Vacuum and Pet Bed Medium are set
  as high-return products by the generator; late returns after the window are
  missing.

## 9. Channel, region and segment (Analysis 9)

- Channel shares of net sales: web **48.0%**, mobile_app **38.5%**, marketplace **13.6%**
- Region with the highest net sales: **London (19.3%)**; share from 'Unknown' region: **1.2%**
- Any dimension where order share and revenue share differ notably: **London**
  has 21.3% of orders but 19.3% of revenue (lowest regional AOV, £49.30).
  **Mobile app** orders are worth more than web or marketplace orders (AOV £56.45
  vs £53.69 and £50.02). **Premium** customers are 17.7% of orders but 19.2% of revenue.

## Summary (for the README)

- Net sales were **£114,204.50** across 2,307 completed orders. They peaked in
  **November 2024 (£10,908.65)**, and the share of revenue from returning
  customers rose from 37% to 61% between the first and last six months.
- Revenue is concentrated: **26 of 45 products** make up 80% of net sales, and
  one product (Cordless Handheld Vacuum) contributes 15.4%.
- **62% of customers bought again** (premium 75%, budget 53%), and RFM
  champions plus loyal customers (28.5% of customers) generate 54% of revenue.
- The unit return rate is 4.8%, but **Wireless Earbuds (32.6%) and the Cordless
  Handheld Vacuum (21.1%)** combine high sales with high returns.
- Discounted orders have larger baskets before the discount but slightly lower
  checkout value, and the pattern is not consistent across segments, so there is
  **no evidence that discounts raise revenue**. This is an association only.
